Budget audit: does the June 10M-step floor imply overtraining?
30 September 2026

No. The 10M number is a lower bound implied by a public command introduced
after the conference, not a verified paper training budget or optimal stopping
point. With one collector, 2,000 epochs x 10 updates x 500 minimum steps gives
10M steps and 20,000 optimizer updates. Four collectors give 40M steps and the
same 20,000 optimizer updates. Collectors finish episodes, so actual counts
exceed both floors. The horizon-80 upper bounds are 11.58M and 46.32M.

This change affects batch size and sample use. Matching samples alone does not
match optimizer updates: at approximately 10M steps, the present four-collector
jobs have approximately 5,000 updates rather than the one-collector recipe's
20,000. The exact counts for each window are in windows.csv/results.json.

I recomputed existing PP/PCP SoftRole logs at fixed 10M and 20M endpoints. Each
number below averages the last 50 complete epochs ending at/before the budget,
weights their episodes equally within each training seed, and then gives equal
weight to each of three seeds. Windows span roughly the preceding 1M steps.
These are training measurements, not frozen-policy evaluation results.

Task/model          Length near 10M   Length near 20M   Reduction
PP shared                    4.840            4.784       1.16%
PP banked                    4.874            4.797       1.59%
PCP shared                  10.692            7.001      34.52%

All 12 individual training seeds have lower episode length at 20M than at 10M.
PP shows small gains after 10M, which can justify calling later training less
cost-efficient for nominal performance. PCP shows substantial continued gains;
stopping at 10M would remove much of its observed improvement. Success remains
very high: 20M equal-seed success is 99.9786% PP shared, 99.9997% PP banked,
99.9952% PCP shared, and 99.9894% PCP banked. Tiny success fluctuations prevent
claiming monotonic improvement in every metric.

The latest tails also have shorter lengths in all 12 seeds than their 20M
windows, but these are not a matched-budget ranking. In particular, the latest
PCP shared mean combines one seed near 20M and two near 40M. At the latest common
PP budget, 37M, shared is 4.741 and banked 4.753. The latest common PCP budget
across all six runs, rounded down to 0.5M, remains 20M.

The structured legacy PCP records reach 10M for all three seeds. Its similarly
weighted mean length near 10M is 24.094, with 99.1545% success. Legacy PP does
not reach 10M in all three available structured ledgers, and none of the legacy
PP/PCP ledgers reaches 20M. Later legacy stdout cumulative counters overcount,
so no exact 20M or latest sample comparison is fabricated. The legacy PCP number
uses the currently reproduced, defective observation path; it is not a fair
estimate of publication-era HetNet performance or an architectural ablation.

Longer training is not itself evidence of overfitting. These logs show no
aggregate episode-length degradation between the tested budgets. They cannot
establish whether transfer/generalization deteriorates while training-domain
performance improves. That requires fixed checkpoints, independent frozen
evaluation scenarios, multiple training seeds, and a checkpoint/stopping rule
chosen without tuning on the final held-out compositions. Existing training
means cannot be directly compared with the publication's evaluation means.

Practical implication: retain the current logs as a separate experiment. For
the reconstructed reference, explicitly record collectors, batch floor,
optimizer updates and actual environment samples. Report both sample-matched
and update-matched comparisons, with predeclared checkpoint budgets. A 10M
checkpoint is useful; 10M should not be retroactively declared the required
stopping point. PP could support a smaller future nominal-training budget if
independent evaluation confirms the small remaining gain. The PCP evidence
does not support that conclusion at 10M. FC is excluded from this performance
interpretation because its known simulator defects remain a separate problem.
